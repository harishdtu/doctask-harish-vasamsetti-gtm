# SuperDocs --- Task 3: Use Cases & Buyers

**Scope note:** This document maps use cases and names companies and
roles only. No named individuals, no contact details, no private
information appear anywhere below. Nobody was contacted in the course of
this research --- every source is a public page (a careers site, an
official association site, or an organization's own published material).
SuperDocs does all outreach itself, always; this is a map, not an
outreach list.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Executive Summary

**Strongest use cases.** Two stand out on evidence strength and buyer
specificity: **government/public-sector proposal response** (GovCon
integrators like ICF International and Parsons, buyer = Proposal
Manager, channel = APMP) and **commercial real estate lease
administration** (CoreWeave, VTS, McCall & Almy, buyer = Lease
Administrator, timely because AI-driven data-center leasing is currently
accelerating this exact document workflow). Both have first-party
evidence (direct job postings on the companies' own careers domains) and
a real, verifiable, buyer-populated professional community.

**Strongest buyer types.** The recurring pattern across every strong use
case is an **operations-titled role inside a document-heavy function**
--- Proposal Manager, Legal Operations Manager, Lease Administrator,
Grants Manager, Commercial Lines Account Manager --- not a C-suite title
and not a generic department name. These are the people who personally
do the copy-edit-reformat work, not people who delegate it.

**Strongest company segments.** Mid-market,
professional/technical-services-adjacent organizations with a document
that must be re-customized per customer, per deal, or per funder. Very
large, highly resourced companies (e.g., Wells Fargo, OpenAI) show the
same document pain in their own job postings but are less plausible
buyers --- they already run dedicated internal platforms for it (see
confidence notes per use case).

**Biggest uncertainties.** (1) Whether any of the named candidate
companies currently use a competing point solution --- none of this
research could observe that, since it isn't publicly disclosed; every
"candidate company" entry is a hypothesis about fit, not a claim about
current tooling. (2) Whether SuperDocs' Word-centric, general-purpose
editing model fits verticals that have already standardized on a
different document paradigm entirely (see the rejected market below).
(3) Company size vs. plausibility as an actual buyer --- several of the
best-evidenced companies (ICF, Parsons, Wells Fargo, CoreWeave) are
large enough that they may already have bespoke tooling; the mid-market
companies in each use case (CleanCapital, Hilb Group, 1st Source Bank,
McCall & Almy) are the more realistic near-term targets even though the
evidence trail is thinner for them.

**Recommended first research/conversation priorities.** In order: (1)
GovCon proposal response --- the evidence is strongest and APMP is a
large, well-organized, easily observable community; (2) CRE lease
administration --- timely, and CoreWeave's posting suggests a company
scaling this function right now; (3) legal contract operations --- CLOC
is a real, sizeable community, and mid-market companies like
CleanCapital are visibly standing up the function for the first time,
which is exactly the "switching trigger" moment worth being early to.

## Research Method

**How companies were selected.** Each use case started from a
document-workflow hypothesis (a document type + an industry where that
document recurs across customers/deals/funders), then was tested against
public job postings, company help-center or "about" pages, and --- where
they exist --- the company's own process documentation. A company only
became a "candidate company" in this document if a public source
described the actual recurring document task, not just the industry in
general.

**Evidence standard.** Every company-level claim below is labeled FACT,
INFERENCE, or UNKNOWN. FACT means a public source directly states it
(quoted or closely paraphrased, with a source URL). INFERENCE means it's
a reasonable read of the FACT but not directly stated. UNKNOWN is stated
plainly rather than guessed.

**What counts as a strong signal.** A named company's own careers page
or official site describing the actual document task in its own words
--- not a generic industry description, not a third-party template
vendor's marketing copy, not a single ambiguous job-board snippet with
the employer name redacted.

**What was excluded.** Job-board aggregator snippets that redacted the
employer name; template-vendor marketing pages used as if they were
company evidence; any source that would have required inferring a named
individual's identity or reaching into gated/private data. One full use
case (technical/API documentation) was investigated and excluded --- see
"Markets investigated and rejected" below --- because the evidence
pointed the opposite direction from the hypothesis.

**Privacy boundary.** No individual name appears anywhere in this
document, including in source material where individual names were
visible (e.g., testimonial attributions were stripped down to title +
company only). No personal email, phone number, or private LinkedIn data
was collected or used.

**Fact vs. inference methodology.** Per use case, the "Evidence" field
states only what a source says. The "Why they care" and "SuperDocs fit"
fields are explicitly framed as hypotheses using words like *likely*,
*appears to*, *would be worth validating* --- never asserted as
confirmed fact about a specific company's internal process or tooling.

## Detailed Use Cases

### Use Case 1 --- Government/Public-Sector Proposal Response

**Problem** Government contractors and professional-services firms
responding to RFPs must produce large, multi-volume Word documents
assembled from a shared "boilerplate" content library (past performance
write-ups, resumes, standard narratives), customized per solicitation,
under hard submission deadlines.

**Current workflow** Proposal teams maintain a content library and route
drafts through "color team" reviews (pink/red/gold), largely in
Microsoft Word, with a proposal manager enforcing formatting and
compliance against the RFP's Section L/M requirements.

**SuperDocs fit** AI editing that lands inside the Word document itself,
rather than a separate chat window a writer must copy text back from,
would remove a step from a workflow this industry already runs almost
entirely in Word --- this is a hypothesis; SuperDocs' actual fit against
Section L/M compliance-matrix workflows specifically would need
validation with a real proposal team.

**Buyer role** Proposal Manager / Director of Proposal Operations. This
role personally owns the content library, drafts non-technical sections,
and enforces formatting --- the pain is direct, not delegated.

**Why they care** The role is explicitly measured on win rate and
submission timeliness under fixed deadlines; anything that reduces the
manual editing/reformatting load between drafting and a compliant final
document is a direct efficiency lever for the metric they're evaluated
on.

**Evidence** - FACT: ICF International has multiple live, named Proposal
Manager postings on its own careers site (careers.icf.com), for its
Energy/Environment/Infrastructure and State & Local business lines.
\[https://careers.icf.com/us/en/job/R2403109/Proposal-Manager-Energy-Environment-Infrastructure\] -
FACT: Parsons Corporation has multiple live Proposal Manager postings on
jobs.parsons.com describing ownership of "the pursuit lifecycle, from
kick-off to final submittal," compliance matrices, and "graphics and
desktop publishing teams."
\[https://jobs.parsons.com/jobs/proposal-manager-infrastructure-north-america-critical-infrastructure-remote-virtual-r-160766-jobs--business-development--\] -
FACT: OpenAI's own careers listing for a federal-facing Proposal Manager
explicitly names "a content library, templates, style guides" as core
deliverables of the role, confirming this pattern extends beyond
traditional GovCon primes.
\[https://jobs.thrivecap.com/companies/openai/jobs/74241957-proposal-manager\] -
INFERENCE: Multiple concurrent open Proposal Manager roles at one
company likely signal proposal volume outpacing current team capacity
--- a plausible switching trigger.

**Candidate companies** ICF International (publicly traded, \~$2B
revenue, environment/energy/health consulting for government); Parsons
Corporation (publicly traded, \~$6B revenue, infrastructure/defense).

**Why each company fits** Both are large enough to run a dedicated
proposal-operations function (evidenced by naming the function and
hiring multiple specialists into it) but are professional-services
firms, not tech platform companies --- plausibly without a bespoke
internal document-editing tool already built, unlike a company such as
OpenAI.

**Where the buyer listens** FACT: APMP (Association of Proposal
Management Professionals, apmp.org) is a real, dues-based membership
association specifically for "bid, proposal, business development,
capture and graphics professionals," with regional chapters, a "Body of
Knowledge," certification programs, and an annual Bid & Proposal Con
conference. \[https://www.apmp.org/, https://www.apmp.org/about/\] This
is a buyer-populated community (the members are the practitioners), not
a vendor-only space, though chapters do partner with vendors for some
events.

**First-conversation hypothesis** *Trigger:* A company posts 2+
concurrent Proposal Manager / Proposal Coordinator roles referencing
"content library" or "boilerplate" maintenance. *Opening question:* How
does your team currently move a first draft from a writer's notes into
the final, formatted proposal document? *Why credible:* Every sourced
job posting above independently describes this exact step as a core,
named responsibility of the role. *What not to pitch immediately:* Any
claim about compliance-matrix automation or Section L/M parsing --- not
a verified SuperDocs capability.

**What we know vs. infer** Know: the role exists, is measured on
proposal throughput, and the core workflow runs in Word with a shared
content library. Infer: that the copy/reformat step specifically (rather
than the writing itself) is the friction point worth solving --- not
directly stated by any source.

**Confidence:** High (best-evidenced use case in this document)

### Use Case 2 --- Certificate of Insurance & Renewal Proposal Customization

**Problem** Commercial-lines insurance agencies process a high volume of
certificates of insurance, endorsements, and renewal proposals --- each
one a document customized with policy-specific terms, additional-insured
wording, and client-specific requirements --- largely by hand inside an
agency management system plus Word/PDF documents.

**Current workflow** An account manager or dedicated CSR reviews the
underlying policy, drafts or edits certificate/endorsement language to
match contractual requirements (e.g., waiver of subrogation,
primary-and-non-contributory wording), and issues the document, often
same-day.

**SuperDocs fit** INFERENCE: in-document AI editing could reduce the
manual drafting/reformatting step for the non-routine certificate
requests (waivers, project-specific wording) that agencies' own
materials say fall outside self-serve portals --- this is a hypothesis,
not validated against agency workflows directly.

**Buyer role** Commercial Lines Account Manager / Agency Operations
Lead. This role personally drafts and issues the documents; it is not a
delegated function.

**Why they care** Certificate turnaround time is a client-facing service
metric ("issue the COI the same day as requested," per one sourced
posting) --- reducing manual editing time directly affects a KPI this
role owns.

**Evidence** - FACT: Marsh McLennan Agency's own job posting for a
Commercial Assistant Account Manager lists "Prepare Proposals for
renewal terms" and "Issue Certificates of Insurance" as core
documentation duties.
\[https://builtin.com/job/commercial-assistant-account-manager/7559858\] -
FACT: Hilb Group's Associate Account Manager posting (King of Prussia,
PA) describes processing "insurance certificates, endorsements, and
correspondence" with same-day issuance expectations.
\[https://builtin.com/job/associate-account-manager/7803906\] - FACT:
Kelly Insurance Group's own public COI guide states that most
certificates are self-serve through a client portal, but anything
involving "waiver wording, primary non-contributory references,
project-specific descriptions... comes through the service team" ---
direct first-party evidence that non-routine certificates are a manual,
human-drafted task.
\[https://kellyinsurancegroup.com/certificates-of-insurance/\] - FACT: A
Hotchkiss Insurance (Houston, TX) posting for Account Associate,
Commercial Lines lists processing certificates as a named duty.
\[https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/account-associate-commercial-lines-houston-tx--81d8d9df-6a6d-427b-977d-1624dbef894f\]

**Candidate companies** Marsh McLennan Agency (the middle-market arm of
Marsh McLennan --- distinct from the giant Marsh McLennan Companies
brokerage, and a more plausible buyer at the agency-branch level); Hilb
Group (a national roll-up of independent agencies); Hotchkiss Insurance
(large independent Texas agency).

**Why each company fits** All three are named, real,
independent-agency-model organizations (not carriers) where the
account-manager role directly produces the documents --- the exact
profile the Kelly Insurance Group source describes as the non-self-serve
segment of certificate work.

**Where the buyer listens** FACT: The Independent Insurance Agents &
Brokers of America (IIABA, "the Big I," independentagent.com) is a
national trade federation of independent agencies with state affiliates
in all 50 states, representing agents and brokers directly (not carriers
or vendors). \[https://www.independentagent.com/,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Insurance_Agents\_%26_Brokers_of_America\]
This is a buyer-populated channel.

**First-conversation hypothesis** *Trigger:* An agency posts multiple
concurrent commercial-lines account manager/CSR roles emphasizing
certificate/endorsement volume. *Opening question:* For certificates
that need custom wording beyond a standard ACORD 25, how does that
request move from the policy file into the final document today? *Why
credible:* Kelly Insurance Group's own public page independently
confirms this split between self-serve and manually drafted
certificates. *What not to pitch immediately:* Nothing about
carrier-side policy issuance --- SuperDocs' fit here is squarely on the
agency side, not the underwriting/carrier side.

**What we know vs. infer** Know: non-routine certificates are manually
drafted by an account manager, and this is a named, time-pressured
service duty. Infer: that in-document AI editing (vs. the current
portal-plus-manual-drafting split) is the right fit --- not confirmed by
any agency source.

**Confidence:** High

### Use Case 3 --- Contract Playbook & Redlining for a First-Time Legal Operations Hire

**Problem** Mid-market companies standing up a dedicated
legal-operations function for the first time need to build and enforce
contract playbooks, templates, and standard operating procedures --- a
document-heavy build-out that previously lived informally in individual
lawyers' files.

**Current workflow** INFERENCE (not directly observed): manual creation
of playbooks/SOPs in Word, likely built by adapting outside-counsel or
prior-employer templates, redlined and iterated as the function matures.

**SuperDocs fit** INFERENCE: in-document AI editing could help a small,
newly-formed legal-ops team draft and iterate playbook language directly
inside the working document, without a separate chat-drafting step ---
not validated with an actual legal-ops team.

**Buyer role** Legal Operations Manager / Head of Legal Operations.

**Why they care** FACT-adjacent: one sourced posting explicitly lists
"Create and maintain standard operating procedures, playbooks, and
training materials for the legal team" as a core responsibility --- this
is squarely document-production work owned by the role.

**Evidence** - FACT: Radian Group's Legal Operations Manager posting
(Wayne, PA) states this is "the company's first dedicated legal
operations professional," reporting to the SVP Deputy General Counsel.
\[https://www.indeed.com/q-contract-legal-operations-manager-jobs.html
--- sourced listing for Radian\] - FACT: CleanCapital's Legal Operations
Manager posting states the role is "responsible for designing,
implementing, and continuously improving the operational infrastructure"
and separately lists creating "standard operating procedures, playbooks,
and training materials for the legal team" as a named duty.
\[https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Legal-Operations-Manager/--in-New-York\] -
INFERENCE: A company explicitly hiring its "first" legal-ops
professional is, by definition, building this infrastructure from a low
base --- a plausible early-adoption window for new tooling.

**Candidate companies** Radian Group (publicly traded mortgage/title
insurance company, Wayne PA); CleanCapital (privately held clean-energy
asset developer/investor, NY, \~$1.5B in investments per its own site).

**Why each company fits** Both are mid-market, non-Big-Law-adjacent
companies explicitly building a legal-ops function now, rather than an
established function that may have already settled on tooling --- a more
credible "switching" window than a mature legal-ops team.

**Where the buyer listens** FACT: CLOC (Corporate Legal Operations
Consortium, cloc.org) is a real, dues-based community explicitly for
in-house legal operations professionals, with an "Individual Member"
category reserved for people who provide legal-ops support directly to
their employer, distinct from an "Ecosystem Member" category for
vendors/consultants/law-firm lawyers.
\[https://cloc.org/who-joins-cloc/,
https://hq.cloc.org/membership-information\] **Distinction worth
noting:** CLOC's main annual conference (Global Institute) is a mixed
vendor+buyer space; local chapter meetings and Individual-Member-only
content are closer to pure buyer participation.

**First-conversation hypothesis** *Trigger:* A company posts its
first-ever dedicated legal-operations role. *Opening question:* As you
build out playbooks and SOPs for the first time, what's the current
process for turning a draft into something the rest of the legal team
actually uses? *Why credible:* Both sourced postings name playbook/SOP
creation as an explicit, current responsibility. *What not to pitch
immediately:* Contract Lifecycle Management (CLM) platform replacement
--- that's a different, more entrenched competitive category (e.g.,
Ironclad, Juro) that SuperDocs is not positioned against.

**What we know vs. infer** Know: both companies are actively building
legal-ops document infrastructure right now, and playbook/SOP creation
is a named duty. Infer: everything about their current drafting process
and appetite for a new tool --- not observed.

**Confidence:** Medium-High

### Use Case 4 --- Statement of Work (SOW) Generation for IT/Technology Consulting & Staffing

**Problem** IT staffing and consulting firms deliver every engagement
under a Statement of Work customized to that client's scope, timeline,
and deliverables --- a recurring, per-deal document-drafting task.

**Current workflow** UNKNOWN in detail for any specific named company;
industry-wide template resources (ClickUp, Smartsheet, Proposify, etc.)
confirm this is treated as a standardized-but-customized document across
the consulting/IT-services industry broadly.

**SuperDocs fit** INFERENCE: per-deal SOW customization is plausibly a
copy-edit-reformat task similar to the proposal use case above, but this
was not confirmed by any named-company source describing the manual
editing step specifically.

**Buyer role** Professional Services Operations Lead / Delivery
Operations Lead (title varies by firm; not confirmed for a specific
named company in this pass).

**Why they care** UNKNOWN specifically; INFERENCE by analogy to Use Case
1 (proposal ops) that turnaround time and consistency across SOWs would
be the operative KPI.

**Evidence** - FACT: ProFocus Technology's own website describes
delivering projects "according to a Statement of Work contract," paired
with a Professional Services Agreement, across engagements ranging "from
one consultant to large teams," with worked examples describing specific
client projects.
\[https://www.profocustechnology.com/statement-of-work-sow/\] - UNKNOWN:
ProFocus's site does not describe how SOWs are drafted or customized per
engagement --- this is the least-directly-evidenced use case in this
document.

**Candidate companies** ProFocus Technology (Nebraska-based IT
staffing/consulting firm).

**Why each company fits** It is a real, named company whose own site
confirms an SOW-driven delivery model --- but the fit claim here rests
more on industry-wide pattern-matching (multiple template vendors
selling IT-specific SOW templates) than on direct company evidence of
the editing pain.

**Where the buyer listens** UNKNOWN --- not independently verified in
this research pass. A plausible candidate (not confirmed) would be a
staffing-industry association; see Use Case 6 for a verified
staffing-industry channel (American Staffing Association) that likely
overlaps with this buyer population, though SOW-specific participation
was not separately confirmed.

**First-conversation hypothesis** *Trigger:* UNKNOWN --- no specific
observable public trigger identified for this use case. *Opening
question:* How much of your SOW is boilerplate vs. rewritten per client,
and where does that rewriting happen today? *Why credible:* Lower ---
this question is templated from the pattern in other use cases, not from
direct evidence about ProFocus or any peer firm's actual process. *What
not to pitch immediately:* Anything implying SuperDocs handles
contract/legal terms --- SOWs often carry binding legal language, and
that portion should stay a legal-review step regardless of tooling.

**What we know vs. infer** Know: the delivery model is SOW-based. Infer
nearly everything else about the editing workflow --- this is explicitly
the weakest-evidenced use case retained in this document (retained
because the industry-wide pattern is strong even though company-specific
evidence is thin).

**Confidence:** Medium (weakest of the retained use cases --- flagged
honestly rather than dropped, since the industry-wide signal is real
even if company-specific evidence is thin)

### Use Case 5 --- Grant Proposal & Funder Report Customization

**Problem** Nonprofits pursuing multiple foundation, corporate, and
government funders must customize a grant proposal (and later a report)
per funder's specific format and requirements, while reusing a large
amount of standard organizational language.

**Current workflow** FACT (from industry sources, not a single named
org): nonprofit grant writers maintain a "boilerplate" or "institutional
language" document and manually adapt sections (mission statement,
program description, budget narrative) per funder, then track a
submission and reporting calendar.

**SuperDocs fit** INFERENCE: in-document editing could reduce the manual
copy/adapt step between the boilerplate document and each
funder-specific submission --- not validated with a specific nonprofit's
workflow.

**Buyer role** Grants Manager / Director of Development (title varies;
smaller orgs combine both).

**Why they care** FACT: sourced job postings explicitly tie this role to
"securing the institutional funding needed to sustain and grow our
programs" and managing "the full lifecycle of grants... from prospect
research and proposal development to funder cultivation and stewardship"
--- direct ownership of the document-production cycle.

**Evidence** - FACT: Reel Works (NYC-based youth filmmaking nonprofit)
posted for a Grants Manager reporting to the CEO, managing "the full
lifecycle of grants... from prospect research and proposal development
to funder cultivation and stewardship."
\[https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit-job/ef36a90b3a9a4e04894eb739ae060430-grants-manager-reel-works-kings-county\] -
FACT: Heading Home Inc. (Boston housing nonprofit) posted for a Grants
Manager to "write compelling, customized grant proposals and LOIs... to
prospective funders" and "develop and maintain an annual grants
calendar."
\[https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit-job/b071b6f3da2a439ba7c3f89667b5ec58-grants-manager-heading-home-inc-boston\] -
FACT: Neighborhood Housing Services of New York City posted for a Grants
